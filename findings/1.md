# Finding #1: DC Coefficient Optimization

**Date:** 2026-01-05
**Status:** Verified across multiple corpora

## Summary

Lowering the DC quantization coefficient from 16 to 6 in mozjpeg's ImageMagick/Robidoux tables provides a significant perceptual quality improvement with modest size increase.

## Results (20 images, Q50)

| Metric | DC=16 (mozjpeg default) | DC=6 (optimized) | Change |
|--------|-------------------------|------------------|--------|
| Avg SSIM2 | 61.38 | 64.22 | **+2.84** |
| Avg bpp | 0.440 | 0.467 | +6.1% |

### Per-image statistics:
- Images improved: 18/20 (90%)
- Images regressed: 2/20 (10%)
- Improvement range: +1.21 to +7.60 SSIM2
- Regression range: -1.74 to -1.91 SSIM2

## Why This Works

The DC coefficient (position 0,0) represents the **average brightness** of each 8x8 block. Preserving this value with higher precision has outsized perceptual impact because:

1. **Human vision is sensitive to luminance**: Average block brightness affects perceived contrast and detail
2. **DC errors propagate visually**: Quantization errors in DC create visible "blocking" artifacts
3. **Low cost, high benefit**: DC is a single coefficient (1/64), so precision increase has minimal size impact
